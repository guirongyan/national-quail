About Q-Storm

The Q-Storm workshop brings together researchers to chart a realistic, evidence-based path for applying quantum computing and quantum sensing to the prediction of severe convective storms (SCS) — one of the most computationally demanding challenges in the geosciences.

Rather than assuming quantum advantage for operational SCS forecasting, Q-Storm sets out to build the scientific roadmap that determines where such advantage might realistically emerge, and what it will take to get there.

By treating SCS hazard prediction as a computationally demanding but tractable benchmark problem, the workshop aims to catalyze interdisciplinary collaboration across atmospheric science, quantum information science, AI/ML, applied mathematics, and computational science — work that can extend, in time, to the broader geosciences and beyond.

Workshop Objectives

The objective of this workshop is not to assume quantum advantage for operational SCS forecasting, but rather to establish a realistic scientific roadmap.

That roadmap will:

  1. Systematically identify where in the traditional SCS modeling workflow quantum approaches may offer advantage;
  2. Establish hybrid HPC–Quantum–AI workflows;
  3. Evaluate use-case priorities across near-, middle-, and long-term horizons; and
  4. Develop benchmark problems related to SCS modeling.

Q-Storm seeks to catalyze and illuminate interdisciplinary collaboration spanning atmospheric science, quantum information science, AI/ML, applied mathematics, and computational science — a foundation that can expand with confidence to the entire geosciences and other science and engineering fields in the future.
